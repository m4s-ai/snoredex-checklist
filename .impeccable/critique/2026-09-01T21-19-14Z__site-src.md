## Design specificity

The product semantics are highly specific; the visual language is moderately specific. Research rows, locality, evidence classes, progress exclusions, and private browser state make this unmistakably Snoredex. The large hero, rounded cards, dark teal palette, simple header, stacked controls, and abstract placeholder could still belong to many calm privacy-first catalogue products. “The Calm Collector’s Desk” is present as restraint, but not yet as a memorable collecting environment: there is little tactile sorting, archival, binder, or collection-specific composition beyond the copy.

## Overall assessment

The strongest qualities are trust, legibility, and restraint. Nunito Sans suits the friendly-but-careful tone; the warm cream/dark ink palette, softened teal actions, restrained shadows, and native controls are comfortable for long sessions. The hero and progress panel create a clear top-level hierarchy.

The main failure is semantic hierarchy. The initial collection instruction is bordered in coral as though something failed, while every populated item shows `Retry save` before any visible error. Those signals overpower the true primary task and weaken the promise of reliable local persistence.

The information architecture is truthful but not sufficiently task-shaped. The landing page moves from one strong CTA to 16 ungrouped localization links and raw catalogue hashes. The working view moves from six possible filters to repeated mini-forms containing status, quantities, note, retry, evidence details, and image inspection. Browser evidence makes the cost concrete: English renders 121 item rows in a document 57,651 px tall. At 390 px wide the page reflows without horizontal overflow and controls remain 44–46 px high, so the problem is density and navigation rather than basic responsive breakage.

Accessibility fundamentals are strong: semantic headings, skip navigation, native selects/radios/details, visible focus, 44 px targets, reduced-motion and forced-colors support, meaningful placeholder alt text, and non-color research cues. The detector found five advisory-only design-system deviations: four off-ramp font sizes (`0.75rem` once and `0.85rem` three times) and one undocumented `rgb(0 0 0 / 55%)` color. Its HTML parser dependencies were unavailable, so it used regex fallback; these are an undercount, not a clean bill of health.

## Cognitive load

**High: 5 of 8 checks fail.** Structural grouping, progressive disclosure, and the hero’s single focus pass. Chunking, visual priority, one-thing-at-a-time interaction, minimal choice, and working-memory demands fail.

- The landing page exposes 16 sibling localization links; the localization selector has 17 choices, and English exposes roughly 50 sets.
- Advanced filtering adds search, status, item class, and research to localization and set, producing six simultaneous criteria plus submit.
- A current-known item offers four statuses, two quantity inputs, note, retry, details, and image inspection—roughly ten decisions in every repeated row.
- Users must retain distinctions among current-known, secured, research exclusion, item class, and finish semantics while editing.

## Emotional journey and peak–end

The opening is calm and confident: “Your Snorlax collection. One checklist.” states the promise immediately. The emotional valley arrives after the hero, where a localization directory and technical fingerprints replace momentum with catalogue administration.

The collection’s first screen creates unnecessary anxiety: a neutral instruction resembles an error, and the named search action is hidden. Populated progress restores some confidence, but “0 of 101” beside repeated `Retry save` controls makes persistence appear broken. There is no strong peak or rewarding end state; on a 121-row page, backup and catalogue information come after an enormous scroll, so the journey ends in maintenance rather than confidence or completion.

## What is working

- **Trust boundaries are designed into the interface.** Research rows are visually distinct, read-only, and excluded from progress without inventing collection truth.
- **Accessibility is structural, not decorative.** Native semantics, focus treatment, target sizes, reflow, reduced motion, forced colors, and alternative text form a robust base.
- **The visual tone fits the product.** Nunito Sans, warm neutrals, restrained teal, and modest depth support careful collecting without gamification or childish franchise mimicry.
- **Responsive fundamentals hold up.** The collection view becomes a coherent single column at 390 px with no horizontal overflow and preserves usable control heights.

## Priority issues

### 1. [P1] Normal states look like failures

**Evidence:** `.state-panel` applies the danger treatment to “Choose a localization or search,” and each item exposes `Retry save` beside an empty live-status region.

**Impact:** Phantom errors directly undermine the app’s core promise of trustworthy local state.

**Direction:** Give the initial instruction a neutral/primary-soft empty-state treatment. Render error copy and retry only after an actual failed save, with the cause in the adjacent live region.

**Suggested next command:** `$impeccable harden site-src`

### 2. [P1] Search is hidden when the interface tells users to search

**Evidence:** The initial collection screen says “Choose a localization or search,” but search only appears inside the closed “More filters” disclosure.

**Impact:** The first instruction points to an invisible action, especially obstructing users who do not know a locality or set name.

**Direction:** Promote one always-visible “Search cards, sets, or numbers” field above secondary filters, or make the instruction explicitly name “More filters.”

**Suggested next command:** `$impeccable clarify site-src`

### 3. [P1] Repeated item rows are too dense for the core collecting action

**Evidence:** Each of 121 English rows exposes four radio choices, two quantities, note, retry, details, and image inspection; the rendered page reaches 57,651 px.

**Impact:** Marking a physical card becomes repeated mini-form parsing instead of a quick status decision.

**Direction:** Make status the compact primary action. Reveal quantities only for Have/Ordered, keep note/evidence progressive, remove idle retry, and validate whether collectors need compact density or batch updates.

**Suggested next command:** `$impeccable distill site-src`

### 4. [P2] The Persuade surface becomes a directory after its hero

**Evidence:** Sixteen localization links dominate one half of the split surface while the other half holds only a short paragraph and substantial empty space.

**Impact:** The product stops building desire before showing the practical proof that makes this checklist distinctive.

**Direction:** Show a grouped localization preview and link to the full collection. Use the freed space for a synthetic collection snapshot, private-state proof, or evidence-class example.

**Suggested next command:** `$impeccable redesign site-src`

### 5. [P2] Provenance is accurate but presented at the wrong level

**Evidence:** Full fingerprint, producer revision, and byte digest are always visible on the landing page.

**Impact:** Deployment-level data interrupts the persuasive flow and makes a collector companion feel like an integrity console.

**Direction:** Lead with a human receipt such as “Catalogue verified · data as of 26 Aug 2026,” then disclose the immutable contract, commit, fingerprint, and digest on demand.

**Suggested next command:** `$impeccable clarify site-src`

## Persona red flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** The CTA is clear, but the first collection state looks broken and the promised search field is hidden. Unexplained terms such as current-known, secured, item class, and research placeholder add doubt before the first success.
- **Alex, power user:** A 121-item catalogue is edited one row at a time with no bulk selection, keyboard accelerator, or compact status-only path. Secondary quantities and recovery controls slow the dominant action.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** Reflow and target sizes are sound, but a 57,651 px populated page separates progress, filtering, results, backup, and recovery by many screens. Persistent orientation and faster row actions matter more than further visual decoration.

## Minor observations

- The placeholder imagery is responsible and licence-safe, but its eye-like motif is generic rather than distinctly archival or collector-oriented.
- “Dark theme” reads as an action while its pressed state communicates a state; the copy could make the current mode clearer.
- The large native set selector depends on knowing or scanning a set name/code; search-first navigation would reduce that dependency.
- The raw provenance source URL reads as data rather than a useful linked action.
- The detector’s off-ramp sizes occur in `site-src/styles.css` at lines 386, 488, 519, and 551; the undocumented overlay color occurs at line 645. These are advisories, not blockers, and should be reconciled with `DESIGN.md` rather than changed mechanically.
- The built catalogue payload is substantial (`snapshot.js` transferred about 2.66 MB); this did not break the inspected session, but it is worth measuring on constrained devices before adding more client-side weight.
